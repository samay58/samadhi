This document is authoritative about **Samay’s experience, dissatisfaction, priorities, and desired product outcome**. It is not automatically authoritative about the correct technical solution.

Treat every proposed implementation, threshold, label, and sequencing choice below as a hypothesis until it survives an adversarial review of:

1. the current source code,

### Operating rule

Before changing product behavior:

1. identify the exact build and commit being tested;
2. reproduce the reported behavior on current `main`;
3. capture diagnostics and screen evidence;
4. trace the behavior through state, event, effect, platform callback, and presentation;
5. independently verify or refute the proposed explanation;
6. state the smallest coherent product decision;
7. only then implement.

Instrumentation, build provenance, test fixtures, and environment repair may be changed during the review if necessary to make truth observable.

# 1. Executive synthesis

Samadhi does not primarily need more features. It needs a coherent center.

The current app has serious engineering underneath it: local playlist analysis, Core Motion cadence, a pure reducer, bounded MusicKit rate control, read-back verification, typed import failures, diagnostics, accessibility, and device evidence. The problem is that the user-facing mental model does not reliably convert that sophistication into a simple, trustworthy experience.

Samay’s central complaint is not merely that Auto is inaccurate, Manual is awkward, imports fail, or the UI needs polish. It is that he cannot form a stable answer to these basic questions:

- What rhythm does Samadhi think I am moving at?
- What rhythm does it think this song contains?
- Why are two different tempo numbers visible?
- What exactly does Auto control?
- What exactly does Manual control?
- Why does the wheel stop where it stops?
- Did the app actually change the song, or only change a number?
- Why did my Manual value follow me into another song?
- Why are many tracks unavailable?
- Is the selected music coherent enough for adaptation to work?
- Is the change mathematically correct, audibly correct, and physically useful?
- Which build am I even evaluating?

Until these questions are answered, visual polish can make the app more attractive without making it more legible.

The reset therefore has five priorities, in this order:

1. **Establish truth:** build identity, original analyzed tempo, cadence projection, requested target, commanded rate, MusicKit read-back, effective result, and target error must be inspectable.
2. **Repair the mental model:** Auto, Manual, the primary tempo value, per-song limits, and track transitions must each have one clear meaning.
3. **Make Auto calm and useful:** the app should estimate motion responsively but change the musical target only when a new rhythm is genuinely sustained.
4. **Improve the music supply:** understand import failures, create a reliable local inventory, and later generate a coherent run set around the settled cadence.
5. **Add craft around proven behavior:** explicit exit from the tempo editor, causal haptics, a brief authored completion sound for verified Auto changes, better setup readability, richer finish and summary interactions, and an authored icon.

The north star remains the repository’s original promise:

> The runner starts moving, the music finds a credible relationship to the runner’s rhythm, the interface confirms what happened, and then it gets out of the way.

# 3. Product intent distilled from Samay’s feedback

The following feedback ledger preserves the complete ask. Each implementation task must cite the relevant feedback IDs.

## F-001: This is a core-experience reset

Samadhi is not close to prime time. The last thing it needs is feature accumulation. It needs one cohesive experience that makes sense.

**Required interpretation:** no decorative expansion may become the lead workstream. Behavior, truth, and trust come first.

## F-002: Auto and Manual are conceptually tangled

A low-tempo song can display a cadence-like value near double its musical BPM. A smaller number may show the musical BPM while the larger dial shows a cadence projection or requested target. The numbers are both defensible internally, but their meanings are not legible.

Switching between Auto and Manual does not make ownership or state clear.

**Required interpretation:** the interface is exposing several domain quantities without a runner-facing hierarchy.

## F-003: Per-song limits feel unexplained

The wheel stops, but the person does not understand whether the limit comes from the song, MusicKit, sound quality, cadence range, or a software choice.

**Required interpretation:** do not remove real quality boundaries blindly. First prove the boundary and make its cause intelligible.

## F-004: Manual state carries across songs in a way that feels wrong

A Manual target selected for one song can remain visible when another song starts, even though the new recording has a different source tempo and reachable range.

**Desired behavior:** Manual is a temporary intervention on the current song. The next song should return to Auto unless deliberate user testing proves a better model.

## F-005: Auto should find a rhythm, not chase noise

The desired Auto experience is not literal callback-by-callback mirroring. The app should develop a general sense of the person’s motion, settle around it, and change only when the new motion is sustained enough to matter.

**Required interpretation:** separate motion estimation from product target selection.

## F-006: Manual adjustment is fun and essential

The wheel is worth keeping. Hearing a familiar song move faster or slower can feel playful and instrument-like.

**Required interpretation:** simplify Manual, do not remove it. Make the causal chain obvious and keep the control reversible.

## F-007: The music source is too eclectic for the mechanic

One arbitrary playlist can contain incompatible tempos, weak beats, live material, alternate versions, and abrupt stylistic transitions. This makes adaptive playback harder and can make the entire experience feel random.

**Desired direction:** build a coherent music inventory and, once the core loop works, create an internal run set around a settled cadence.

## F-008: A “warehouse” of the user’s music may be more useful than one playlist

Potential sources include multiple selected playlists, the user’s library, recently played tracks, or Apple Music recommendations.

**Required interpretation:** start with a bounded, incremental, local inventory. Do not synchronously analyze an entire library by default.

## F-009: A generated playlist or run set should be possible

Samay suggested Apple Music playlist creation, or an LLM workaround if Apple does not support it.

**Research result:** MusicKit already supports creating a playlist from music items. An LLM workaround is not needed for the basic write path. Internal queue generation should precede export.

## F-010: Too many tracks appear to fail import

The failures feel random and implausibly frequent.

**Required interpretation:** measure the complete failure distribution before relaxing resolver rules. Distinguish wrong identity, no preview, temporary network failure, decode failure, ambiguous rhythm, low confidence, and no useful cadence projection.

## F-011: Auto does not visibly prove that it works

A slow walk should be useful as a development test, even if walking is not ultimately a production mode. The person needs to see that sensing, target selection, and playback adaptation are alive.

**Required interpretation:** separate product range from calibration range. The current 120 to 210 SPM boundary is an app policy, not a limitation of `CMPedometer`.

## F-012: Meaningful Auto changes should have an authored completion cue

The person needs to know when Samadhi has found a rhythm, committed a new Auto target, applied a Manual target, reached a boundary, or rejected a command.

Samay specifically wants a very short, high-quality sound when Auto has completed a meaningful adjustment, analogous in interaction role to the soft completion cue heard when an agent session finishes in Cursor. The product should borrow that sense of quiet completion, not copy Cursor’s proprietary sound asset.

**Required interpretation:** this Auto completion sound is now an explicit product requirement, not merely an optional hypothesis. It should play only after a meaningful Auto target change has been committed and the corresponding MusicKit rate has been verified by read-back. It must not play for raw cadence callbacks, a merely considered target, every ramp increment, or an unverified command. Pair it with a restrained haptic. Create an original one-shot through a sound-design process such as ElevenLabs Sound Effects, or source a properly licensed high-quality asset, then trim, mix, and master it locally for Samadhi.

## F-013: The tempo editor traps transport

After opening or manipulating the wheel, pause and next may remain inaccessible until the control times out.

**Desired behavior:** add an explicit close or back action. Closing the editor must immediately restore transport without discarding the current Manual target.

## F-014: Setup readability and motion need refinement

The playlist page can feel dark, mechanical, and insufficiently inviting. Analysis progress could feel more crafted.

**Required interpretation:** fix contrast and hierarchy before adding expressive motion. Avoid decorative smoke or running graphics until they prove a clear role.

## F-015: Transport and hold-to-finish need more tactile joy

Pause, previous, next, and finish should feel responsive and native. The existing hold safety mechanic is liked and should remain.

**Required interpretation:** improve touch response, haptics, and morphing without weakening finish safety.

## F-016: The summary needs clearer closure

The summary is directionally sound but basic, hard to read in places, and not emotionally satisfying enough.

**Required interpretation:** improve hierarchy and completion, not metric breadth.

## F-017: The icon should be more personal and authored

The current icon can be more distinctive, colorful, and related to the product’s rhythm-and-stillness idea.

The dictated tool name was unclear. Recraft is a plausible interpretation but not confirmed.

**Required interpretation:** use generative tools only for exploration. Final artwork should be deliberately selected, redrawn or refined as layered vector art, and completed in Apple Icon Composer.

## F-018: The development environment is not reliably portable

Much of the work occurred on the Mac mini. The Mac mini and the primary Mac must both be able to reproduce, test, sign, install, and inspect the project.

**Required interpretation:** environment reproducibility is part of the product program, not housekeeping.

## F-019: “Original BPM” and the complete tempo transformation are hidden

Samay can hear that tempo changes, but cannot independently determine whether the song’s source tempo is correct, whether the half-time or double-time interpretation is correct, whether the required rate was calculated correctly, or whether MusicKit applied it.

**Required interpretation:** build a Tempo Truth mode before redesigning the core semantics.

## F-020: The app must distinguish mathematical, platform, perceptual, and embodied success

A changing number is not enough. A successful loop requires:

1. correct source analysis,
2. correct cadence relationship,
3. correct rate command,
4. correct MusicKit read-back,
5. audible and acceptable playback,
6. a useful bodily relationship to motion.

# 4. Independent research findings and their implications

The agent must independently recheck these findings against the SDK installed on the working machine. They are the current research baseline, not a substitute for local interface inspection.

## 4.1 Apple Music can create playlists

MusicKit exposes `MusicLibrary.createPlaylist(name:description:authorDisplayName:items:)`. It also supports editing playlists created by the app. It does not allow an app to rebuild arbitrary playlists created by another app.

**Implication:** build an internal Run Set first. Add explicit “Save to Apple Music” later. Store the identifier of any Samadhi-created playlist if repeated edits are desired.

**Verdict on the prior LLM workaround:** rejected as unnecessary for playlist creation. An LLM may later support taste curation, but it is not required for the mechanical write path.

## 4.2 The user’s library can be queried and paginated

`MusicLibraryRequest` supports limits and offsets, and Music item collections expose subsequent batches. Songs, tracks, playlists, and playlist entries conform to library request protocols.

**Implication:** a music warehouse is technically possible. The current app’s one-page playlist request is not sufficient for a complete library index. A production warehouse must paginate, deduplicate, persist incremental progress, and remain useful while partially analyzed.

## 4.3 Apple Music does not expose an authoritative BPM field for catalog songs

The documented Apple Music song attributes include title, artist, album, artwork, duration, ISRC, previews, and other catalog data, but not tempo.

**Implication:** “Original BPM” must be labeled **Analyzed source tempo** unless corroborated. Do not present an estimator result as Apple-provided truth.

## 4.4 Preview audio is available, full-stream PCM is not

MusicKit exposes preview assets and preview URLs. It exposes streamed playback through `ApplicationMusicPlayer`, but not a documented PCM callback, DSP graph, beat grid, downbeat map, or full-track file URL for subscription playback.

**Implication:** the current preview analysis is a reasonable scalar-tempo input, but it cannot establish full-track beat phase. The preview and full stream must not be assumed to have identical edit boundaries or phase.

## 4.5 `playbackRate` is writable, but its quality envelope is undocumented

`MusicPlayer.State` exposes a writable `playbackRate`. Apple does not document a supported musical range, pitch-quality guarantee, or artifact threshold for streamed Apple Music playback.

**Implication:** 0.90 through 1.10 is an empirical Samadhi envelope, not a platform guarantee. Keep or change it only through blinded, route-specific listening evidence.

## 4.6 Tempo match is not beat lock

Research using D-Jogger found that continuous tempo matching alone produced limited phase synchronization, while strategies that established and maintained beat-to-footfall phase alignment produced much stronger synchronization. Running studies also show that small tempo changes can influence cadence without being consciously obvious.

**Implication:** Samadhi currently supports tempo relationship, not footfall-phase lock. Do not claim “beat sync,” “in phase,” or a true per-step match. The product can still be valuable, but the claim boundary must remain explicit.

## 4.7 Step-based pacing is more stable than stride-based pacing when synchronization occurs

Research comparing step-based and stride-based auditory pacing found more stable auditory-motor coordination with step-based pacing.

**Implication:** retaining explicit one-step-per-beat and two-steps-per-beat relationships is defensible, but the app must not silently relabel musical tempo. The runner-facing cadence target and the source musical tempo must remain separate truths.

## 4.8 Walking cadence is available from Core Motion

`CMPedometerData.currentCadence` reports steps per second and is described as live pedestrian data. The current app rejects values below 120 SPM because of its own running envelope.

**Implication:** walking can be supported in a development calibration harness immediately. Production walking support remains a product decision and needs musical-quality testing.

## 4.9 Haptic clicks fit a dial, but overuse weakens them

Apple’s guidance explicitly identifies click feedback as useful for dial increments and warns that overuse or overlapping clicks can make it confusing. Custom haptics can vary sharpness and intensity, with lower sharpness conveying a softer, more organic feel.

**Implication:** keep causal detents, major intervals, one boundary stop, and one ownership transition. Do not add haptics to every internal Auto callback.

## 4.10 Liquid Glass belongs primarily to controls and navigation

Apple’s guidance says Liquid Glass should define a control or navigation layer above content, not become the content layer itself. Native glass is appropriate for interactive buttons, transient controls, and morphing control groups. It is not a reason to turn a progress rail or passive status block into glass.

**Implication:** improve the analysis progress through typography, motion, shape, and truthful state. Reserve glass for Start, transport, tempo controls, close, and finish.

## 4.11 The final icon workflow should use Icon Composer

Apple’s current icon workflow uses layered source artwork, vector or transparent raster layers, and Icon Composer for Default, Dark, Clear, and Tinted appearances.

**Implication:** Recraft or another generator may help produce concept families. It should not be the final craft process. Final concepts must survive small-size, monochrome, wallpaper, and multi-appearance tests.

## 4.12 Independent tempo tools require license discipline

Librosa is available under the permissive ISC license and can serve as a development-only independent cross-check. Essentia is AGPL, aubio is GPL, and madmom’s pretrained model/data files carry noncommercial restrictions even though much of its code is BSD.

**Implication:** do not add an external analyzer to the shipping app during this reset. A development-only Python validation script using Librosa is the lowest-friction independent comparator. Preserve Samadhi’s zero third-party production dependency unless a later decision explicitly changes it.

## 4.13 Original short sound effects can be generated offline and bundled locally

ElevenLabs Sound Effects supports text-described sound-effect generation, explicit duration control, and non-looping WAV output. Apple’s Core Haptics APIs can also register a bundled audio file as a custom audio resource and schedule it with a haptic event. These capabilities make it practical to create and prototype an original Auto completion cue without adding a runtime generation service.

**Implication:** use ElevenLabs, bespoke sound design, or a properly licensed library only as an offline asset-creation path. Verify current rights before shipping, preserve provenance, bundle the selected final asset locally, and physically test the playback mechanism alongside `ApplicationMusicPlayer`. Do not assume that a technically playable cue will mix correctly with Apple Music, preserve the route, avoid ducking, or feel good under real songs.

# 6. Provisional product model

This model is the best synthesis of Samay’s intent and the evidence. The agent must challenge it, then either adopt it or record a better alternative.

## 6.1 Separate the system into seven layers

### Layer 1: Source rhythm evidence

What the recording appears to contain:

- analyzed musical BPM,
- alternate supported pulse,
- confidence,
- analyzed preview duration,
- analyzer version,
- evidence provenance,
- variable or ambiguous tempo status.

### Layer 2: Motion observation

What the phone currently observes:

- raw Core Motion cadence,
- sample age,
- callback interval,
- filtered cadence,
- acquisition or tracking state.

This layer should be responsive and honest. It should not be artificially sluggish merely to keep the product calm.

### Layer 3: Settled Auto target

What Auto currently trusts enough to use as the musical target:

- acquiring,
- settled,
- considering a new target,
- committed change,
- confidence lost.

This layer creates calmness. It should not mirror every filtered cadence update.

### Layer 4: Track fit

Which explicit cadence projection of which ready track can serve the settled target with the least distortion and discontinuity.

### Layer 5: Fine rate control

The bounded playback-rate command, MusicKit read-back, latency, effective musical tempo, effective cadence pulse, and residual error.

### Layer 6: Transport authority

Previous, Skip, and player-confirmed natural boundary change the current song. Planning can prepare, but cannot silently change, the current song.

### Layer 7: Presentation and feedback

One runner-facing target, one ownership model, one visible status, causal haptics, and optional development disclosure.

These layers must not be collapsed into one `bpm` field.

## 6.2 Auto

Provisional meaning:

> Auto follows the runner’s settled cadence and organizes the music around it.

Auto should:

1. observe filtered cadence;
2. establish a settled target;
3. ignore ordinary variation;
4. consider a change only after a meaningful sustained shift;
5. commit the new target once;
6. ramp the current song within its proven envelope;
7. prepare a better future track when the current song cannot fit;
8. remain the default at run start and at every confirmed track change.

The current user-facing Auto correction should be removed unless the audit finds a valuable real use case. Internal correction and calibration values may remain in diagnostics.

Do not hardcode the earlier proposed 4 SPM, 6 SPM, 8-second, or 12-second thresholds before replaying real traces. Those are starting test values, not research-backed constants.

## 6.3 Manual

Provisional meaning:

> Manual temporarily sets the target rhythm for the current song.

Manual should:

- begin when the wheel is turned or Manual is explicitly selected;
- use an absolute target, not a hidden offset;
- preserve the runner’s observed cadence separately;
- remain through pause and same-song route recovery;
- remain active after the editor closes;
- clear on Previous, Skip, or natural boundary;
- clear when the user explicitly returns to Auto;
- never carry an old song’s range into the new song.

The Manual override should be track-scoped in domain state.

## 6.4 Primary display

Preferred hypothesis:

> The large number is the target rhythm in **SPM**.

Examples:

```text
164 SPM
Following your stride
```

```text
168 SPM
Manual for this song
```

```text
172 SPM
Settling
```

Do not show a second competing BPM value in the normal run surface merely because it exists in diagnostics.

The tempo editor may provide a subordinate source-tempo disclosure if testing shows that it improves understanding:

```text
Song analyzed at 84 BPM
2 steps per beat
```

This disclosure must not dominate the running experience.

## 6.5 Per-song limits

The current-song range is:

```text
selected cadence projection × proven playback-rate envelope
```

Keep this boundary until the listening gate changes the envelope.

When the wheel reaches it:

- visual travel stops;
- the value stops;
- detent feedback stops;
- one terminal haptic occurs;
- repeated outward motion is silent;
- reversing responds immediately;
- copy explains “This song’s limit”;
- Auto may show that it is preparing a better next fit.

A limit must never feel like a dead control or hidden error.

## 6.6 Track change

At every player-confirmed change:

1. verify operation and track identity;
2. clear old pending rate work;
3. clear track-scoped Manual ownership;
4. preserve the settled Auto target;
5. derive the new track’s cadence projection;
6. calculate and command the new rate;
7. wait for MusicKit read-back;
8. show a brief Auto confirmation;
9. reject stale old-track callbacks.

## 6.7 Tempo Truth mode

The normal product should be simple. Development must be exhaustive.

For the active track, Tempo Truth must expose:

```text
Build commit
Track identity
Analyzed source tempo
Alternate pulse
Confidence
Analyzer version
Evidence provenance
Selected step-beat relationship
Original cadence projection
Raw cadence
Filtered cadence
Settled Auto target
Active target
Required rate
Commanded rate
MusicKit applied rate
Command latency
Effective musical BPM
Effective cadence pulse
Residual target error
Verification status
```

Example:

```text
Analyzed source tempo       84.0 BPM
Relationship                2 steps per beat
Original cadence pulse      168.0 SPM

Settled Auto target         175 SPM
Required rate               1.0417×
Commanded rate              1.042×
MusicKit applied rate       1.040×

Effective musical tempo     87.4 BPM
Effective cadence pulse     174.7 SPM
Target error                -0.3 SPM
Status                      Verified
```

Use “Analyzed source tempo,” not “Original BPM,” unless the value has independent corroboration.

## 6.8 Walking

Do not expand the production promise merely to make debugging easier.

Add a development calibration mode that can:

- accept walking cadence,
- show raw and filtered sensor data,
- use known low-tempo material,
- demonstrate the complete rate calculation,
- save traces.

After the test, decide whether warm-up walking belongs in the product.

## 6.9 Music Warehouse and Run Set

Do not use “random songs from everything.”

A Music Warehouse is a local, incremental, deduplicated inventory of analyzed tracks and their source provenance.

A Run Set is an internal queue selected from that warehouse around:

- settled target SPM,
- cadence fit,
- required stretch,
- analysis confidence,
- adjacent pulse continuity,
- artist repetition,
- source diversity,
- optional taste signals.

The first Run Set planner should be deterministic. No LLM is required.

Export to Apple Music is optional and comes after the internal set passes listening review.

# 7. Proposed domain seams

These names are illustrative. Preserve semantics, not spelling.

```swift
enum RhythmOwnership: Sendable, Equatable {
    case automatic
    case manual(ManualRhythmOverride)
}

struct ManualRhythmOverride: Sendable, Equatable {
    let trackID: MusicTrackID
    var targetSPM: Int
}

struct AutoTargetState: Sendable, Equatable {
    var observedCadenceSPM: Double?
    var settledTargetSPM: Double?
    var candidateTargetSPM: Double?
    var candidateDurationSeconds: Double
    var secondsSinceCommit: Double
    var status: AutoTargetStatus
}

enum AutoTargetStatus: Sendable, Equatable {
    case acquiring
    case settled
    case considering
    case confidenceLost
}

enum TempoEvidenceSource: String, Codable, Sendable {
    case samadhiAnalyzer
    case independentAnalyzer
    case humanTap
    case publishedReference
    case humanVerified
}

struct TempoEvidence: Codable, Sendable, Equatable {
    let source: TempoEvidenceSource
    let bpm: Double
    let alternateBPM: Double?
    let confidence: Double?
    let analyzedDurationSeconds: Double?
    let analyzerVersion: Int?
}
```

### Required invariants

- Manual always names the track it applies to.
- A confirmed track change invalidates Manual.
- Observed cadence is never overwritten by a Manual target.
- Settled Auto target changes only through the Auto target policy.
- Source musical BPM is never relabeled as cadence.
- Applied means verified MusicKit read-back.
- Planning never becomes transport authority.
- Views never write player or motion state directly.
- A stale callback cannot alter a replacement run or replacement track.
- The summary never infers a match from intent alone.
- The visual aperture never claims audible beat phase unless phase is actually measured.

## Phase 3: Causal feedback and supporting craft

### FEEDBACK-301: Create the Auto completion cue and the broader haptic grammar

#### Product decision

Ship one brief, authored sonic cue for a **verified meaningful Auto change**. Its interaction role should resemble a refined task-completion sound: Auto has found and successfully applied the new rhythm, so the system gives one quiet moment of closure.

The cue is not a metronome, sensor tick, wheel detent, notification ding, or generic success chime.

#### Exact trigger contract

The success cue must occur only when all of the following are true:

1. the settled Auto target has materially changed;
2. the reducer has committed that new target;
3. the player has received the resulting rate command;
4. MusicKit has reported a matching applied-rate read-back for the current session, operation, request, and track;
5. the effective cadence pulse is inside the accepted match tolerance, or the Auto policy has reached the verified nearest truthful boundary it intentionally selected.

Do not play the cue when:

- raw or filtered cadence changes;
- Auto is merely considering a candidate target;
- a rate ramp takes an intermediate step;
- a command remains pending;
- MusicKit rejects or mismatches the command;
- stale feedback arrives;
- the current target is reaffirmed without a meaningful change.

A rejected command receives a separate restrained warning haptic, not the completion sound.

#### Sound brief

Generate and audition an original family of short one-shot candidates. The starting brief, subject to physical testing, is:

- roughly 180 to 450 milliseconds, with no conspicuous long tail;
- soft but decisive;
- warm, rounded, lightly tonal or tactile-percussive;
- audible over music at low level without masking the song;
- emotionally closer to “settled” or “complete” than “alert” or “reward”;
- clean in mono on the iPhone speaker and through Bluetooth headphones;
- no hard UI click, bright notification bell, coin sound, arcade reward, stock cursor sound, or obvious sample-pack character.

Borrow the emotional function of Cursor’s completion cue, not its literal waveform.

#### Candidate creation and provenance

Create at least 12 candidates, then narrow to three physical prototypes. Candidate sources may include:

1. ElevenLabs Sound Effects, used offline to generate original one-shots with controlled duration;
2. bespoke synthesis, Foley, or recording created for Samadhi;
3. a high-quality sound library with a license that clearly permits app distribution and modification.

For every candidate preserve:

- source or generator;
- prompt or recording notes;
- license and usage terms as of acquisition;
- raw file;
- edits and processing chain;
- final file checksum.

The final sound must ship as a bundled local asset. Do not add an ElevenLabs API call, network dependency, or generated-at-runtime audio path to the product.

#### Playback implementation review

Independently test the smallest reliable mechanism for playing the bundled cue alongside `ApplicationMusicPlayer`, including a custom Core Haptics audio resource or a local AVFoundation player. Do not choose the mechanism from theory alone.

The selected implementation must not:

- change or reactivate the audio session in a way that pauses music;
- duck Apple Music unless explicitly selected and approved;
- create a route change;
- interrupt background playback;
- produce a start-up delay on the first cue;
- desynchronize the haptic and sound;
- fail silently when haptics are disabled or unavailable.

#### Broader event grammar

Continue to prototype restrained haptic treatment for:

- cadence acquired;
- Manual detent;
- Manual target verified;
- current-song limit;
- rejected command;
- Return to Auto;
- run finish.

The Auto completion sound is the one required sonic event in this phase. Other sounds require separate evidence before inclusion.

#### Acceptance

- Samay can identify that Auto has completed a real change without looking in at least four of five trials;
- the cue never fires before verified read-back;
- intermediate rate steps produce no repeated sound;
- the cue remains pleasant and non-fatiguing across at least ten meaningful Auto changes;
- it is audible but unobtrusive across five representative songs;
- it causes no perceptible music duck, pause, gap, route glitch, or background failure on the iPhone speaker and primary Bluetooth route;
- sound and haptic feel causally simultaneous;
- functionality remains understandable with sound or haptics disabled;
- Samay explicitly selects the final candidate on the physical phone.

# 15. Final product test

The reset succeeds when Samay can say, without seeing a debug panel:

> I start moving. Samadhi finds my rhythm without twitching. When Auto completes a meaningful verified adjustment, one subtle authored cue lets me know the new rhythm has settled. The music changes in a way I can hear and feel. I can turn the wheel for the current song, understand the boundary, close it, and get back to transport. The next song returns cleanly to Auto. The music selection feels coherent. When something fails, the app tells the truth. When the run ends, the summary feels clear and earned.

And when debugging, the team can answer every one of those feelings with evidence:

> This was the analyzed source tempo. This was the supported cadence relationship. This was the settled target. This was the required rate. This was the command. This was MusicKit’s read-back. This was the effective result. This was the remaining error. This was the track transition authority. This is the exact build that produced it.

That combination, an effortless surface supported by ruthless internal observability, is the standard for Samadhi’s next phase.
